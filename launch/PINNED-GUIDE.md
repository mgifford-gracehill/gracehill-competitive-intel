## The ten-second version

A competitor comes up on a call. You search their name and read the line at the top of their
page. That's it — that's the tool working as designed.

Everything else is there for when you have more than ten seconds.

| You have | Use |
|---|---|
| **Ten seconds, mid-call** | Search the name. Read the top line. |
| **Ten minutes, prepping** | Open the competitor page — how we win, what to ask, what never to say. |
| **A champion to arm** | Download the battlecard. Send page two. |
| **A problem, not a name** | Type what the buyer actually said into the search box. |

## The four tabs

**Start here** — this week's watch, the 30-second pitch, when to use which message, and which
battlecard to download. If you only ever read one page, read this one.

**Competitors** — all 98, each with one label saying how to play it: *Head-to-head*, *Sometimes
in the room*, *Not our fight*, *Partner*. Filter, or select several to compare.

**Explore** — five ways in when you don't have a name: by outcome, by problem (the buyer's own
words), by persona, by product line, or a straight capability comparison.

**What's new** — the weekly digest, plus what buyers named in live deals.

**Ask / report** sits in the top bar on every page.

## The three battlecards

One button, one dropdown, on every competitor page.

| Card | When |
|---|---|
| **Sales** | A competitor is in a live deal and you have a call tomorrow. Two pages — the second is written for your champion to read without you there. |
| **Product** | You're in a demo or an RFP and need to be precise about what they actually do. |
| **Account management** | A customer is being courted, or you're cross-selling. Written to protect revenue, not win it. |

They generate from the live database, so a card you download today reflects what we know today.
Nothing goes stale in a drawer.

## Two things that will save you

**Some competitors are also partners.** Yardi, Entrata, ResMan and Anyone Home, Knock, Funnel,
ChatMeter and EliseAI are all listed partners at gracehill.com/partnerships — and some of their
products still compete with ours. Those records carry a warning at the top. The rule: **compete on
the product in front of you, never on the company.** A rep running down Yardi in a room where the
customer runs Yardi PMS has just damaged two relationships in one sentence.

**Sales and account management are not the same message.** On a new logo you're the outsider and
the status quo is the opponent — you disrupt it. On a renewal *you are the status quo*, and
disrupting it means displacing yourself. Corporate Visions' research puts a number on it: using
an acquisition-style message on an existing customer raises the odds they leave by at least 10
percent. Start here has the full frame.

## The weekly digest, and how it relates to this

Every Monday a digest posts to **#industry-competitive_intel** (sales-facing: what to say) and
**#industry-industry_intel** (company-wide: what's happening in the industry). Those are
deliberately short — three to five competitor items, and no more than three per section on the
industry side. A digest nobody finishes is worth less than a short one everybody reads.

**The app has considerably more.** Everything the weekend sweep found, the full record for all 98
competitors, and the updates that didn't clear the bar for a company-wide post. The digest is the
headline; this is the detail behind it. If a digest item makes you curious, the competitor's record
is where the rest of it lives.

## Fact-checking content before it publishes

There's a Claude skill called **competitive content review**, posted in the beta channel. Download
the file and add it in Claude — or if IT has already published it to our shared skill library, just
switch it on there.

Point it at any piece that names a competitor — blog post, landing page, one-pager, ad, deck,
webinar script, comparison page, press release — and it checks every claim against this same
database, then tells you what to change and why.

**Please run it on anything competitor-facing before it goes out.** It exists to catch the two
things that cost us most:

- a claim about a competitor we can't source, which reads as credible to someone who hasn't checked
  and as unreliable to the buyer who has — and the buyer for a comparison piece always has tabs open
- a comparison that's legally risky, usually an absolute negative ("they can't do X") with no named
  public source behind it

## What it is not

- **Not customer-facing.** Nothing here goes in front of a buyer as-is. Verify against CRM and
  win/loss first.
- **Not a replacement for Salesforce or Gong.** It doesn't track your deals. It tells you what to
  say in them.
- **Not a rumor mill.** Every competitor claim needs a public source. We never publish a
  competitor's price, customer count or headcount unless they publish it themselves, and we never
  say a competitor *can't* do something without a named source.
- **Not complete, and that's deliberate.** *Unknown* is a real answer and appears as one. Never
  present an Unknown to a buyer as a No.

## How it stays current

A research sweep runs every weekend across all 98 competitors, the trade press, and regulatory
sources. It also reads what buyers said about competitors in live deals.

Facts — ownership, funding, launches, published pricing — update automatically with an
*unverified* flag. Anything that's a judgment call — positioning, why we win, a competitor's
weakness — queues for human approval and doesn't appear until it's approved. **Nothing reaches a
Slack channel without a person saying yes.**

Records show when they were last reviewed. If a date looks old, say so.

## How to contribute

Use **Ask / report** in the top bar. Competitor pages also have a "Something wrong here?" button
that fills in which record you were on.

The four most useful things you can send:

1. A competitor we don't track that showed up in your deal.
2. A claim in here you don't believe, or couldn't defend.
3. A question you couldn't answer on a call.
4. A section that didn't earn its space.

Please don't name a customer or prospect in a report — describe the situation instead. This feeds
a shared record.

**Anything wrong can be overridden on the back end.** The records are editable and a correction
takes minutes, so don't leave something alone because it looks baked in. Flag it — that's faster
than working around it, and the next person on that call gets the fixed version.
