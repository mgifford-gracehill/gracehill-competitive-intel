The 30-second pitch

Data can show where performance is slipping. Grace Hill gives teams a clear path to resolution. For multifamily teams, that means turning property data, benchmarks, and market context into actions that improve the resident, prospect, and employee experiences behind portfolio results — NOI, revenue, retention, and time to lease.

The new bottleneck is resolution.

The resolution loop

1. READ

Pull property, lease, resident, team and market context together.

2. DIAGNOSE

Correlate it to find the cause, not the symptom.

3. PRESCRIBE

Name the next best action for a specific property.

4. ASSIGN

Put it in the hands of the person who owns it.

5. PROVE

Re-measure to confirm the outcome actually moved.

Steps 3, 4 and 5 are the ones nobody else does. That's the whole argument. Say ASSIGN, not push-into-your-PMS — deeper two-way write-back is roadmap. Describe assignment to the person who owns the action; do not promise automated write-back into third-party systems.

What good would look like

1. One picture

People, resident experience, and market in one view — not four reports.

2. A named next action

Not just what happened. What to do about it, at that property.

3. One standard

Holds at every property, not only where a strong manager happens to be.

4. Proof it worked

Measured against benchmarks, in numbers your exec team already uses.

This is the hinge of the whole pitch. If they agree with this list, they have agreed to purchase criteria only we meet. Do not move on without an explicit yes.

The moat

Evidence: Surveys, Shops, Reviews, Market data, Property performance
Context: Benchmarks, Market comps, Portfolio trends, Industry expertise
Improvement: Training, Policies, Onboarding, Guided action, Proof of impact

A model can be rebuilt. Years of what happened next cannot.

Avoid saying 'more data'. Say we connect evidence, context, and improvement on the same properties over time.

Where AI fits

What AI changed. Finding problems is becoming nearly free. Any tool can now tell you a property is underperforming, or that a review was negative. Detection is no longer scarce.

What it hasn't. Knowing the right fix, getting it done, and proving it worked. That needs an owned library of remediations and years of outcomes to compare against — not a better model.

More findings just means more to resolve. And whether the person in front of your resident is on payroll or is an agent, someone still has to decide what good looks like.

Fair questions

“We already get some of this free in our PMS.”
You do — and you should use it. Free modules record activity. They don't benchmark it or tell you which property to fix first.

“This is a lot to take on at once.”
Then don't. Start with one track where the budget already sits. Expansion is a tier change, not a new project.

“Different teams own these budgets.”
That's the whole problem — and it's why we priced it so one team can start without waiting on the others.

“We're not replacing our system of record.”
Good — don't. It stays the ledger. We sit above it and hand work back to the people who do it.
