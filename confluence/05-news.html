This week's talk track

Two things landed in the last three weeks that change your prospect's risk math. First, a watchdog group filed 114 separate Section 8 discrimination claims against Greystar across six states and DC — built entirely on recorded undercover calls to leasing offices. Greystar's public defense was, essentially, "we provide training." Second, HUD is simultaneously gutting the nonprofit fair housing groups it funds, which means private testers and plaintiffs' firms are now the primary enforcement channel — and they don't have a 100-day deadline or a budget cap. The question to ask an ops leader this week isn't "do you train on fair housing?" It's "if someone recorded 40 calls into your leasing offices tomorrow, could you prove what your team was taught, when, and that they passed?" That's the gap the training-plus-policy-plus-mystery-shop stack is built to close — documented competency, current policy, and independent evidence of what's actually being said on the phone.
