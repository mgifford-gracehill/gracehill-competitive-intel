## 1. Scope: federal yes, state and municipal no
*Set by Mandy Gifford, 2026-08-17.*

**Grace Hill does not handle state or municipal rulings.** Federal rulemaking and
enforcement — HUD, DOJ, Fair Housing Act, Title VI — is in scope. State statutes,
city ordinances and local enforcement actions are not something Grace Hill's policy
or training content is maintained against.

What this means when writing the digest:

- **Still report state and municipal news** when it is genuinely newsworthy. It shapes
  the buyer's risk picture and reps should know what their accounts are facing.
- **Do not imply Grace Hill covers it.** Never write a "so what" or talk track that
  suggests Grace Hill tracks, supplies or keeps content current for state or local
  rules, or that a rep should sell on "we'll keep you current with your local
  ordinance." That is a promise the product does not make.
- **Do not anchor the weekly talk track on a state or municipal ruling.** Lead on
  federal action, or on operator execution risk that is not jurisdiction-specific:
  training, documented acknowledgment, consistent on-site behavior, proof of who
  was taught what and when.
- **When a local rule is the news, frame the "so what" around what the operator has to
  do** — retrain staff, re-document the policy, prove acknowledgment — not around
  Grace Hill supplying the local legal content.

Worked example. On the Seattle fee ordinance (2026-08-13), this is wrong:
> "Every fee policy and leasing script in a Seattle portfolio has to change" — implies we supply the rewrite.

and this is right:
> "Operators holding Seattle assets have to change fee policies and retrain staff before July 2027. The question for them is whether they can show which version of a policy each associate acknowledged, and when."

## 2. Never name a Grace Hill customer or prospect
Applies to prose **and to source URL slugs**. Press-release URLs routinely carry the
operator's name even when the summary does not. Strip the link and write
"(source link withheld)", keeping the competitive point. Route the item to the
account team via the owner-only DM.

---

## 3. Never invent a fact, URL, date or number
If the batches did not verify it, it does not go in. "Nothing found" is a useful answer;
a padded list is not.

## 4. The two channels — what goes where
*Set by Mandy Gifford, 2026-08-17.*

There are two Slack channels and they get two different digests from the same weekend sweep.
Do not cross-post. Do not send one digest to both.

**#industry-competitive_intel** (`CD5JVAM4P`) — sales-facing. Answers *what do I say*.
Contains: the talk track, competitor moves, and field intel from deals.

**#industry-industry_intel** (`C0226FATTED`) — company-wide, anyone at Grace Hill.
Answers *what is happening in our industry and what does it mean*.
Contains: regulatory and enforcement, industry research, market data, and target-account signals.

The channel's own description sets the boundary: "Share information/news/insights about our
industry. Please keep this channel industry specific. Refer to #industry-competitive_intel
for competitor news/insights."

**The industry digest is not a pitch.** Its readers are colleagues, not prospects, and many
are not in sales. No talk track, no "here's how to sell against this," no product mentions
unless the news genuinely concerns a Grace Hill product. If a regulatory item has a clear
sales implication, that belongs in the competitive digest's talk track, not here.

Where an item is genuinely both, put the detail in the industry digest and let the
competitive digest reference it in one line.

## 5. Language: write outside-in, and cut the jargon
*Set by Mandy Gifford, 2026-08-17. Source: the Punchy messaging framework, Dunford
positioning, and the current Pitch deck and Discovery deck.*

### The order to write in

Lead with what it means for the reader. Then what they can now do. The mechanism last, or
not at all.

- ❌ Inside-out: "HUD issued a proposed rule modifying disparate impact analysis under 24 CFR Part 100."
- ✅ Outside-in: "The federal standard for proving discrimination is loosening — which means state law, and what your own teams actually say on a call, become the exposure."

The reader is the hero of every item. Grace Hill is at most the tool, and in the industry
digest usually not present at all.

### Open with the shift, not the headline

The strongest weekly openers name a change in the world and what it sets up, rather than
recapping an article. One paragraph. No preamble.

### Words to cut

| Don't write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| signals · "signals, connected" | evidence, context and improvement — or just name the thing: survey scores, shop results, reviews |
| more data | what the data lets someone *do* |
| actionable insights | say what the action is: "which properties need attention, and why" |
| comprehensive platform | list what is connected: "training, policy, surveys and benchmarks in one place" |
| streamline | name the process: "cut onboarding time," "standardize policy reviews" |
| drive results | name the result: "improve shop scores," "reduce turnover," "shorten time to lease" |
| best-in-class | ground it: "built by people who came out of property management, updated when the law changes" |
| trusted partner | earn it with a number: tenure, renewal rate, response time |
| performance solutions (as a label) | describe the outcome |
| leverage, utilize, robust, seamless, holistic, unlock, empower, transform | use, strong, works with, whole, let, help |
| AI as an employee | who is interacting with your residents — human or agent |
| push into your PMS · automated write-back | ASSIGN — the work goes to the person who owns it |
| "Operations" as a standalone persona | owners and operators, property teams, regional leaders |

### Tests to run before sending

1. **The so-what test.** Every item ends with a consequence for the reader. If the "so what" restates the summary, it isn't one.
2. **The specificity test.** A number, a name, a date, or a deadline in every item. "Significant growth" fails; "up 19% year over year" passes.
3. **The read-aloud test.** If you would not say it to a colleague in a hallway, rewrite it.
4. **The delete test.** Cut every sentence that survives being deleted. Short digests get read.
