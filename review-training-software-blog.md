# Competitive review — "Best Property Management Training Software 2026"

Checked against the competitive intelligence database, 12 August 2026.
Competitors named in the post: Yardi Aspire, RealPage EasyLMS, GROW LMS, Innform.

**Summary:** 3 of the 4 competitor sections contain claims the database explicitly flags as
things not to say, because a prospect with a browser can disprove them in about ninety seconds.
One competitor is characterized as the opposite of what it is. One isn't in the database at all.

Severity key — **Fix** means it is factually wrong or contradicts a published source.
**Soften** means it is unverifiable rather than untrue. **Check** means someone needs to confirm a
Grace Hill claim before this publishes.

---

## 1. Yardi Aspire — four claims the database says not to make · **Fix**

This is the section carrying the most risk. Aspire is our most credible head-to-head competitor,
and every claim below is contradicted by Yardi's own published pages.

### 1a. "Training content is lighter" / table: "Moderate; relies on 3rd-party content"

Aspire ships its own real estate course library. Published, verifiable specifics:

- Fair housing courses approved by **TDHCA (Texas)** and the **Florida DBPR**, qualifying for CE credit
- State-variant harassment prevention courses for **California, Connecticut, New York, Maine and Illinois**, with stated ongoing regulatory updates
- **100+ bilingual English/Spanish courses**, plus Canadian French
- Supports **NAA credential maintenance** (CAM, CAPS, CALP)

> **Replace with:** "Aspire's library is real — state-approved fair housing content in Texas and
> Florida, state-variant harassment courses, and 100+ bilingual courses. Yardi does not publish a
> total course count, so depth is hard to compare line for line."

### 1b. "Aspire relies on 3rd-party courseware for property management-specific compliance" · **Fix**

Not supported by any public source. Their fair housing and harassment content carries their own
state approvals. Delete this sentence.

### 1c. "Built for general HR and employee development, not property management operations" · **Fix**

Aspire is an LMS built *by a property management software vendor* and sold with a real estate
course library. This is the single most disprovable line in the post.

### 1d. "When regulations change, you wait for Yardi's schedule, not your industry's" · **Fix**

Their state harassment course pages state ongoing regulatory updates. Delete.

### What to say instead — all publicly verifiable

These are the real, defensible gaps:

- **Capability is split across three purchase tiers.** SCORM compatibility, embedded e-signature,
  live polling and Microsoft Teams integration are named as **Platinum-tier** features. The base
  tier is not the same product — worth asking which tier a quote actually covers.
- **No training benchmarking against an industry dataset** is described on the Aspire product page
  or catalog overview.
- **No published course count**, so library depth can't be compared from public sources.
- **Fair housing approvals named publicly are Texas and Florida only** — no broader multi-state
  approval list is published.

> **Suggested replacement for "Where it falls short":** "Aspire's capability is split across three
> purchase tiers — SCORM, e-signature, live polling and Teams integration sit in the top tier, so
> the entry product isn't the one in the demo. Yardi publishes fair housing approvals for Texas and
> Florida; no broader multi-state list is public. And nothing on the Aspire page describes
> benchmarking training performance against an industry dataset, so you can see completion but not
> how you compare."

## 2. RealPage "EasyLMS" — the product name is out of date · **Fix**

RealPage's current product page is titled **RealConnect**. EasyLMS survives only in older customer
video collateral. Publishing a comparison against a name they've moved away from dates the piece
immediately, and hurts SEO for the term buyers actually search.

> **Rename throughout:** "RealPage RealConnect (formerly EasyLMS)" on first mention, then RealConnect.

### "Courseware is basic and generic" / "Limited compliance coverage" / "No PM-specific content depth" · **Soften**

The RealConnect product page is **silent** on fair housing, state-specific variants, CEC
eligibility, SCORM import and benchmarking. Silence is not absence, and the database flags
"RealPage has no fair housing training" as a claim not to make.

What RealConnect *does* publish, which the post omits and a prospect will notice:

- Mobile-first design built for just-in-time access
- Learning pathways assignable by position, market type and location
- Fully translated English and Spanish learner interface
- Blended delivery combining self-paced content with live classes

> **Replace "Where it falls short" with:** "RealPage doesn't publish what's actually in the library
> — the RealConnect page describes delivery (mobile-first, pathways by position and location,
> blended live and self-paced) but not fair housing content, state compliance variants, CEC
> eligibility or course count. Ask to see the catalog and the state approval list before you
> assume coverage."

**Also worth knowing, though probably not for this post:** RealPage's parent remains subject to
active antitrust litigation over algorithmic rent-setting. Relevant in a sales conversation,
inadvisable in published marketing.

## 3. GROW LMS — characterized as the opposite of what it is · **Fix, highest priority**

The post says GROW is "a general workforce LMS without property management focus," with "minimal
property management content" and "generic compliance modules."

**GROW by Inhabit is multifamily-native.** Published and verifiable:

- **300+ courses across 28+ topic areas** including leasing, maintenance, operations, fair housing and resident retention
- **Fair housing accredited in Texas and Virginia**
- Human trafficking content meeting **Florida and California** requirements
- **Asbestos Class IV** aligned to EPA and OSHA standards
- Courses available in **SCORM** format for delivery inside a client's existing LMS
- Custom course development by property management professionals

The database flags "GROW has no fair housing content" as a do-not-say. The current draft is a
stronger version of that same claim.

**A likely cause worth checking:** there are two similarly named products. **GROW by Inhabit** is
the multifamily one. **isolved Learn & Grow** is a genuinely generic workforce LMS. The
description in the post fits the second and is attached to the first.

> **Replace the whole GROW section with:** "**Best for:** Operators who want multifamily course
> content without changing platforms — GROW sells courses in SCORM format for your existing LMS.
> **Strengths:** 300+ courses across 28+ topic areas, fair housing accreditation in Texas and
> Virginia, and human trafficking content meeting Florida and California requirements. Genuinely
> built for this industry. **Where it falls short:** Published fair housing accreditation covers two
> states. GROW's site doesn't describe CEC eligibility for CAM, CAPS or CALP, bilingual content,
> competency assessment, HRIS integration or benchmarking. And the same capability appears as GROW,
> as ResidentIQ Learning and as ResMan Learning depending which door you come in, which makes it
> hard to know what you're buying."

## 5. Grace Hill's own claims — three to verify before publishing · **Check**

### 5a. Stevie Award year and tier

The post says **"2025 Bronze Stevie Award for LMS."** The product database records a **"2024 Gold
Stevie Award for LMS"** from the American Business Awards. Those may be two separate awards in two
years, but one of them is likely wrong, and Bronze-vs-Gold is not a detail to get backward in a
piece arguing for third-party validation. Confirm with marketing.

### 5b. "600+ courses"

Repeated seven times and load-bearing for the whole argument. I couldn't verify it against a
published Grace Hill source in the database. Confirm the current number against gracehill.com
before this goes out — a competitor fact-checking us would start exactly here.

### 5c. The "only platform" claims · **Soften**

Five separate absolute claims: "the only platform built by property management experts," "the only
platform recognized as," "the only platform that does this at scale," "Grace Hill is the only real
choice," "Grace Hill is the only choice."

The first is the risky one. Both GROW and Aspire are built for this industry by people in it. The
award claims are narrower and probably defensible; the capability claims are not.

> **Suggested:** keep "the only platform recognized as a 2024 NAA Supplier of the Year" if the award
> is confirmed. Change "the only platform built by property management experts, for property
> management" to something you can hold — for example "the only platform that connects training to
> policy, surveys, mystery shopping and reputation in one place," which is true and is the actual
> differentiator.

### 5d. "Competitors: Manual updates. Delayed rollout. Client liability exposure." · **Fix**

An unsourced negative applied to every competitor at once, including two who publish ongoing
regulatory update commitments. This line does more legal work than marketing work.

> **Replace with:** "Ask any vendor how regulatory updates reach you: automatically, on their
> release schedule, or only when you notice and request them. The answers vary more than you'd expect."

## The pattern worth noticing

Every error in this piece runs the same direction: a competitor described as thinner than it is.
That reads as credible to someone who hasn't checked and as unreliable to someone who has — and
the buyer for this post is a training director evaluating all five platforms with tabs open.

The database's Yardi Aspire verdict line is the better strategy, and it's already written:

> "The most credible head-to-head LMS in the category — it has real multifamily content,
> state-approved fair housing, 100+ Spanish courses and AI authoring, so win on outcome proof
> (competency, shops, surveys, benchmarks) rather than on feature checklists."

That's the argument this post could make and currently doesn't: they teach, we prove it worked.
None of the four competitors connects training to a mystery shop, a resident survey and a
benchmark. That's a claim that survives fact-checking.
